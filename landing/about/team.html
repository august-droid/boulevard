About

The people behind Boulevard's writing.

Boulevard is an AI music app, the AI alternative to Spotify. Our articles are written by people with real history in music and tech, not an anonymous content mill. Here is who writes what, and how we keep it honest.

August founded Boulevard in 2025 after a decade in music tech. He writes about AI music, the streaming industry, and why most people in 2026 still don't know there's a better option than Spotify. Based in Copenhagen, Denmark.

August handles the comparisons and the "should I switch" pieces, the questions a normal listener actually asks before changing apps. Recent work: Boulevard vs. Spotify, How to cancel Spotify Premium, Spotify's 2026 pricing, and Boulevard vs. Suno.

Maya covers AI music, vibe-based streaming, and where the two intersect with culture. Former editor at an indie music magazine. Based in Brooklyn. Has strong opinions about Drake.

Maya writes the roundups, the culture pieces, and the honest "is this any good" reviews. Recent work: The best AI music apps in 2026, The best Suno alternatives, Is AI music real music?, and Drake AI songs.

Jordan writes the legal and technical explainer pieces at Boulevard: copyright, royalties, lawsuits, terms of service. Trained as a paralegal. Allergic to buzzwords.

Jordan handles the pieces where getting a fact wrong matters. Recent work: How does AI music work?, Is AI music copyrighted?, Why RIAA is suing Suno and Udio, and Is Suno legal?

How we write these articles

Boulevard is an AI music company, so we will say it plainly: we have a product in this space. We still hold every article to the same rules.

  • Legal claims trace to primary sources. When we write about copyright or lawsuits, we tie it to the U.S. Copyright Office, published court filings, or a company's own terms. We say "alleged" when something is alleged and "no court has ruled" when no court has ruled.
  • We name competitors, and we concede. We compare Boulevard to Spotify, Suno, Udio, and Apple Music by name, and we tell you plainly where they beat us. An article that only flatters our own app is not worth reading.
  • Every article is dated. You see a publish date and an updated date. When the facts move, we move them.
  • Prices and product details change. When we cite a number, we mark it "as of" a date and tell you to confirm it at the source.
  • Plain English. No jargon for its own sake. If a sentence needs a glossary, we rewrite the sentence.
